№ I — My Approach

The language that got you funded is holding you back.

You’re an early-stage tech startup that worked hard on a pre-seed funding pitch. Success! You made it. But when it's time to talk about your product on your website, with potential hires, at networking events, and over dinner, you’re greeted with polite nods, blank stares, and very few conversions.

The problem is startups form their product language in specific, high-stakes contexts: VC pitches, co-founder recruiting, early developer hires. These contexts reward a particular kind of language: technical, precise and appealing to the broadest possible market. Yet this same language fails to scale, and even impedes internal and external growth. It doesn’t speak to your end customer, attract future hires, or form a durable brand or public identity.

If you’re a startup with a complex product — an AI data-scraping API, a vector database — this problem is amplified. Technically impressive and widely applicable, you risk speaking to no one other than angel investors and software engineers. And the problem doesn’t go away at scale, either. Growth-stage companies navigating a strategic pivot face the same deficit with higher stakes: new audiences to reach, new discovery surfaces, and a core message that hasn’t kept pace with the product.

The solution isn’t just language that speaks to your audience, which got you into the problem in the first place. Rather, at an early stage or in transition, startups need to get clear in non-technical terms about what they’re really offering and for whom. This clarity is the key to downstream growth, not only externally — in terms of marketing, communications, and positioning across search, LLM responses, and integration ecosystems (SEO, AEO, GEO) — but internally as well: product, hiring, and organisational cohesion.

Through my Clarity Sessions coaching and advisory support, or in project-based engagements ranging from pitch decks to keynote presentations to web positioning and more, I work with you to first establish this clarity before shaping your messaging for the room. Because you can’t scale what you don’t see.
